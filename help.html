1. I forgot my password or I'm locked out

  1. On the sign-in page, click Forgot password (or Can't access your account).
  2. Enter your work email and follow the code or link sent to your email or phone.
  3. Choose a new password of at least 12 characters. A few random words works well, for example: blue-kettle-orbit-seven.
  4. Sign in again on every device that asks, including your phone's mail app.
  5. If you never receive the code, check your spam folder and wait five minutes before trying again.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if you no longer have access to the recovery email or phone, if the account says it is disabled, or if you did not request the reset. An unrequested reset can mean someone else is trying to get in.

2. My Wi-Fi keeps dropping

  1. Check whether other devices drop too. If only one does, skip to step 5.
  2. Unplug the router and modem, wait 30 seconds, then plug the modem in first and the router a minute later. Wait three minutes.
  3. Move closer to the router for a test. If the connection holds, the signal is too weak where you were.
  4. Check that the router is off the floor, away from microwaves and not tucked inside a cabinet.
  5. On the affected device, open Wi-Fi settings, choose Forget this network, then reconnect with the Wi-Fi password.
  6. Turn the device off and on again.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if it drops several times a day after these steps, if the whole office is affected, or if the router is more than five years old.

3. My printer says it's offline

  1. Make sure the printer is on, shows no error light and has paper.
  2. Check that the printer is on the same Wi-Fi network as your computer, or that its USB cable is firmly plugged in.
  3. Restart the printer, then restart your computer.
  4. Windows: open Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Printers & scanners, select the printer and click Open print queue. Cancel any stuck jobs, then in the Printer menu make sure Use Printer Offline is not ticked.
  5. Mac: open System Settings > Printers & Scanners, remove the printer with the minus button, then add it again with the plus button.
  6. Print a test page.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if the printer still shows offline after being removed and re-added, or if several people can't print.

4. My computer is running slow

  1. Save your work and restart. A computer left on for weeks slows down, and this fixes it more often than anything else.
  2. Close browser tabs you aren't using. Twenty or more open tabs can use most of the memory.
  3. Check free storage space. If less than 10% of the drive is free, delete large files you don't need or move them to cloud storage.
  4. Windows: press Ctrl + Shift + Esc, open Task Manager and look at what uses the most CPU or memory. Don't end anything you don't recognise.
  5. Windows: in Settings > Windows Update, install any waiting updates and restart. Mac: do the same under System Settings > General > Software Update.
  6. Windows: in Task Manager's Startup apps tab, switch off programs you don't need at sign-in.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if it stays slow after a restart and an update, if it makes grinding or clicking noises, or if it is more than five years old with a hard disk rather than a solid-state drive.

5. My email isn't syncing or sending

  1. Check your internet connection by opening any website.
  2. Look in your Outbox folder. A message stuck there is usually a large attachment; remove it or share a link instead.
  3. Close and reopen the mail app, then restart your computer or phone.
  4. Try the same account in a web browser. If the browser works, the problem is the app, not the account.
  5. If the account asks for a password, enter it again. A recent password change is the most common cause.
  6. On a phone, remove the account and add it back.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if the browser version doesn't work either, if you see messages about the account being blocked or over its storage limit, or if mail you send is bouncing back.

6. I can't connect to the VPN or remote desktop

  1. Check your internet connection by opening any website.
  2. Make sure you are typing your username and password exactly as given. Check that Caps Lock is off.
  3. Close the VPN or remote desktop app completely, reopen it and try again.
  4. Restart your computer. If you use a work laptop at home, restart your home router too.
  5. For remote desktop, the office computer must be turned on and not asleep. Ask someone at the office to check, or wake it if you can.
  6. Try a different network, such as your phone's hotspot. If it works there, your home or hotel network is blocking the connection.
  7. If the app shows an update button, install the update and try again.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if you see a certificate, authentication or "server not found" error after these steps, or if no one in the office can connect.

7. A shared drive or network folder is missing

  1. Check that you are on the office Wi-Fi or connected to the VPN.
  2. Ask a coworker whether their shared drive is missing too. If it is, the problem is the server or network, not your computer; call us.
  3. Restart your computer and check again.
  4. Windows: open File Explorer > This PC and see whether the drive letter appears with a red X. Double-click it to reconnect.
  5. If it is gone entirely, click See more (three dots) > Map network drive, pick a drive letter and enter the folder path you were given. Tick Reconnect at sign-in.
  6. If it asks for a username and password, use your work sign-in.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if you don't know the folder path, if it says you don't have permission, or if the whole office has lost the drive.

8. A program won't install or update

  1. Restart your computer and try again. A pending update blocking the install is the most common cause.
  2. Check free storage space. Most installs need several gigabytes free.
  3. Download the installer only from the software maker's official website, never from a pop-up or ad.
  4. Close other programs, including the older version of the one you are installing.
  5. Windows: right-click the installer and choose Run as administrator if you have permission.
  6. Write down any error message or code exactly as shown.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if the installer asks you to turn off your antivirus, if you do not have permission to install software, or if the same error appears twice.

9. I got a suspicious email or clicked a link

If you have not clicked anything:

  1. Don't click links or open attachments, and don't reply.
  2. Look at the sender's real address, not just the name. Look for small misspellings in the domain.
  3. Delete it, or forward it to us if you are unsure. Treat urgent requests for money, gift cards, passwords or changed bank details as a warning sign.
  4. If it seems to come from someone you know, confirm with them by phone or in person.

If you already clicked a link or opened an attachment:

  1. Close the page. Don't type any password or personal information.
  2. If you already entered a password, change it right away, from a different device if possible, and turn on two-step verification.
  3. Disconnect from Wi-Fi if you opened an attachment and anything unusual happened.
  4. Contact us the same day.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 right away if you entered a password, opened an attachment, or sent money or information to the sender.

10. I see a virus warning or a pop-up telling me to call a number

A browser pop-up that says your computer is infected and gives a phone number is a scam. Real warnings do not tell you to call a number.

  1. Don't call the number and don't click anything inside the pop-up.
  2. Close the browser. If it won't close, press Ctrl + Alt + Delete (Windows) or Command + Option + Esc (Mac) and force it to quit. Don't reopen the same tabs when asked.
  3. Windows: open Windows Security > Virus & threat protection > Scan options, choose Full scan and run it.
  4. Remove any browser extension you don't recognise.
  5. Change the passwords for your email and bank if you typed them anywhere during the pop-up.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 immediately if you called the number, allowed anyone to connect to your computer, or paid anything. In that case, turn the computer off, change your passwords from another device and call your bank.

11. A Windows or Mac update is stuck

  1. Wait. Large updates can take an hour or more, and the screen may stay on one percentage for a long time.
  2. Do not turn the computer off while it says Working on updates or Don't turn off your computer, unless two hours have passed with no change.
  3. After two hours, hold the power button for 10 seconds to shut down, wait 30 seconds and turn it on again.
  4. Once it starts, Windows: go to Settings > System > Troubleshoot > Other troubleshooters and run Windows Update. Mac: check System Settings > General > Software Update.
  5. Make sure you have at least 20 GB free before trying again.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if it returns to the same stuck screen, loops through restarts, or shows an error code.

12. I got a blue screen, or my computer won't start

  1. Write down or photograph any error text or code on the screen.
  2. Hold the power button for 10 seconds, wait 30 seconds, then turn it on again.
  3. Unplug USB devices, printers and external drives and try once more.
  4. Check that the power cable is firmly connected at both ends and the outlet works. On a laptop, try with the charger connected.
  5. If it starts normally, save your work and run any waiting updates.
  6. If it shows the same screen or repeats, stop trying and don't keep forcing it off.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if it won't start after these steps, if the blue screen returns, or if you hear clicking or grinding. Back up your files as soon as you can if it starts again.

13. I can't sign in to Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace

  1. Check that Caps Lock is off and retype your email address and password slowly.
  2. Try signing in from a web browser in a private or incognito window. If that works, the problem is a saved sign-in on your device.
  3. If you use two-step verification, check that your phone's clock is set to automatic. Codes fail when the time is wrong.
  4. Sign out of the app, close it, reopen it and sign in again.
  5. Use Forgot password if the password may have changed.
  6. Check the service's status page in case the outage is on their end.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if it says the account is locked or disabled, if you get sign-in alerts you didn't cause, or if several people can't sign in.

14. My sound, camera or microphone isn't working on a video call

  1. In the call app, click the settings or arrow next to the microphone and camera icons and check the correct device is selected.
  2. Check that nothing is muted: the app, the computer volume and any mute button on a headset.
  3. Close other apps that may be using the camera or microphone, then rejoin the call.
  4. Windows: open Settings > Privacy & security > Camera (or Microphone) and make sure access is on for the app. Mac: use System Settings > Privacy & Security.
  5. Unplug and replug any USB headset or webcam, or try another USB port.
  6. Restart the computer.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if the device does not show up at all, or if it works in one app but not others after these steps.

15. My browser shows pop-ups, ads or redirects me to strange sites

  1. Close the pop-ups without clicking inside them.
  2. In your browser's settings, find Notifications under site settings and remove any site you don't recognise.
  3. Open your browser's extensions list and remove any you did not install on purpose.
  4. Clear your browsing data, including cookies and cached files, for all time.
  5. Check your home page and search engine settings. If they have changed, change them back.
  6. Windows: run a full scan in Windows Security.
  7. Restart the browser.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if the problem returns, if your home page keeps changing, or if you see warnings you can't close.

16. A new employee is starting, or someone is leaving

This guide is for the business owner or manager. Send us the request at least two business days ahead when you can.

New employee checklist:

  1. Tell us their full name, start date, job role and which shared folders and programs they need.
  2. We create their email account and a computer login with only the access their role needs.
  3. We set up two-step verification on their account.
  4. We prepare their computer with updates and security software before day one.
  5. On day one, they set their own password and sign in with you present.

Departing employee checklist:

  1. Tell us the date and time they leave. For an unplanned exit, tell us right away.
  2. We disable their account on the day they leave. We disable rather than delete so you can still access their email and files.
  3. We reset their password and sign them out of all devices.
  4. Collect the computer, phone and any keys or cards.
  5. Decide who receives their email and files, and tell us.
  6. Change any shared passwords they knew.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 to start either checklist.

17. I deleted a file, or a file is missing

  1. Stop saving new files to the same drive until you have checked, since new files can overwrite deleted ones.
  2. Look in the Recycle Bin (Windows) or Trash (Mac) and restore the file.
  3. Use the search box in File Explorer or Finder to look for the file name. It may have been moved.
  4. Windows: right-click the folder, choose Show more options > Restore previous versions, and look for an older copy. Mac: open Time Machine if you have it set up.
  5. If you use OneDrive, Google Drive or Dropbox, open the website and check its recycle bin or version history.
  6. Check your email attachments and downloads folder for a copy.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if you can't find it, if the drive shows errors or makes clicking sounds, or if a whole folder disappeared. Do not use the drive until we have looked at it.

18. I got a backup failed alert

  1. Don't ignore it. Until a backup works, your files are not protected.
  2. Check that the backup drive is plugged in and switched on, or that the computer has internet access if you back up online.
  3. Check that the backup drive or cloud account has free space. A full drive is the most common cause.
  4. Restart the computer and run the backup again by hand.
  5. Note the error message and the date and time of the last successful backup.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if the second attempt also fails, if the backup has not worked for more than a week, or if the alert mentions a damaged or unreadable drive.

19. The internet is slow for the whole office

  1. Check whether it is slow on every device and website. If only one site is slow, the problem is that site.
  2. Run a speed test and compare it with the speed you pay for.
  3. Restart the modem and router: unplug both, wait 30 seconds, plug in the modem, wait a minute, then plug in the router.
  4. Ask whether anyone is downloading large files, streaming video or running a backup or update.
  5. Plug one computer directly into the router with a cable. If it is fast, the Wi-Fi is the problem, not the internet service.
  6. Check your internet provider's outage page or call them.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 if the speed is still low after a restart and a cabled test, or if the problem happens at the same time every day.

20. I think we've been hit by ransomware or a data breach

Signs include files that won't open or have strange new endings, a message demanding payment, or a customer telling you they received odd messages from you. Act quickly and call first.

  1. Disconnect the computer from the network now: unplug the network cable and turn off Wi-Fi.
  2. Don't turn it off or restart it, and don't delete anything.
  3. Take a photo of any message on the screen with your phone.
  4. Do not pay or reply to anyone demanding payment, and don't contact the sender.
  5. Warn others in the office to disconnect their computers if they see the same signs.
  6. Call us immediately at 662-402-9032. Don't wait to email.
Call or text us at 662-402-9032 right away. Do not try to fix this yourself. Don't plug in backup drives until we say it is safe, since the backups can be infected too.
